About

My current research is weight-space prediction: training neural networks to generate the weights of other networks. Teaching a model a new task usually means fine-tuning it over thousands of gradient steps. I study whether a second model can learn that process and produce the result directly, from a short description of the task or a handful of examples.

Before this, I was at IBM Research, where I worked on foundation models. I started in drug discovery, on protein language models (MAMMAL) and on diffusion models for structure prediction and design. I then joined the Granite Vision team, which builds the vision-language model of the IBM Granite family. A recurring theme was getting different modalities to work together inside one model.

I did my MSc at Tel Aviv University, on adversarial machine learning and the robustness of face recognition (StyleAT, BMVC 2026).

Can we compress backpropagation into a model?

Fine-tuning teaches a model a new task through thousands of gradient steps. We trained a meta-model to skip them: it reads a one-paragraph description of a task, or a handful of examples, and writes the weight update for a base model in a single pass.

Research

  1. Now

    Weight-space prediction

    Models that generate other models. A meta-model reads a task description or a few examples and produces a weight update for a base model in one forward pass, with no gradient steps. I'm interested in what such a map can learn, why it should exist at all, and where it breaks.

  2. IBM Research, Granite Vision team

    Vision-language models

    I worked on Granite Vision, the vision-language model of the IBM Granite family, built to understand documents, charts and tables alongside text.

  3. IBM Research, drug discovery

    Multimodal integration

    Getting models of different modalities to work as one. I worked on aligning biomedical encoders to large language models so they can reason over proteins and molecules alongside text (BioVERSE), and on fusing protein language models, LLMs and 3D structural models into unified protein representations.

  4. IBM Research, drug discovery

    Foundation models for drug discovery

    Biomedical foundation models across proteins, small molecules and genes: protein language models (MAMMAL), latent-variable transformers for controllable molecule generation (STAR-VAE), and diffusion models for biomolecular structure prediction and design.

  5. MSc, Tel Aviv University

    Adversarial machine learning

    Robustness of face recognition to semantic attacks, where the adversary changes attributes such as age or pose instead of adding pixel noise. We built BoundStyle, an attack in StyleGAN's latent space that runs about 9.5 times faster than earlier attacks, and StyleAT, an adversarial training scheme that uses it to defend against attacks it has not seen.
